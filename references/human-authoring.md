# Human authorship

This is the primary rule set. The participant's own account is the write-up. The skill must not replace it with generated prose.

There is no required voice, structure, or format. Do not impose headings, numbered steps, bullet points, tables, a TL;DR, an introduction, a conclusion, a fixed paragraph count, or a standard order of sections. Let the participant tell the solve naturally.

Use the current solved conversation as the source. Prefer the participant's own sentences, observations, shorthand, commands, and explanations. Keep the original order when it reflects what happened. The skill may remove irrelevant chat noise and omit failed attempts by default, but it must not change the substance.

If a necessary action is missing, ask one neutral question in ordinary language. Ask only what is missing. Do not lead the participant toward an answer or supply the explanation for them.

Examples of useful participant wording:
- `found this string in robots.txt`
- `then i put it in cyberchef`
- `this gave the flag`
- `strings showed this weird key`
- `i changed the cookie and refreshed`

Keep that language. Do not convert it into formal phrases such as "enumerated the attack surface", "leveraged the endpoint", or "subsequently decoded the payload".

The skill must not:
- invent first-person sentences;
- turn assistant recommendations into participant actions;
- generate a polished narrative to fill gaps;
- deliberately add spelling errors or irregularities;
- imitate handwriting or a person's identity;
- optimize wording to evade AI detectors;
- claim the material is human-authored.

If the participant explicitly asks for a spelling correction, make only that correction. Otherwise preserve their wording, including their natural punctuation and shorthand.

Because PinakaCTF requires the final submission to be handwritten and in the participant's own words, any generated document is only a plain evidence/reference copy. The participant must create the actual handwritten write-up themselves.
